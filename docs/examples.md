These examples answer three practical questions:

- what you already have
- what you ask Jini for
- what you get back next

## 1. Turn meeting notes into a sendable follow-up {#meeting-followup}

You paste:

- notes
- loose decisions
- implied owners
- action items that are not clean yet

You ask:

```text
turn these notes into a follow-up I can send
```

You get back:

- `Sendable Follow-up`
- `Owners and Due Points`
- `Task List`

Then you do:

- open `Sendable Follow-up`
- confirm the owners and due points
- review what is still missing
- send or edit with less cleanup work

## 2. Check whether a plan is really ready {#spec-readiness}

You paste:

- a draft
- comments
- a handoff that feels almost done

You ask:

```text
check whether this plan is ready to hand off
```

You get back:

- `Build-Readiness Check`
- `Handoff Brief`
- `Missing Pieces Before Build`
- `Task List`

Then you do:

- open `Build-Readiness Check`
- clear the missing pieces before build
- fix what still blocks the handoff
- avoid sending a plan that only looks finished

## 3. Plan a 7 day Paris trip without losing the thread {#paris-trip}

You paste:

- your travel dates
- rough budget
- what kind of trip you want
- anything you already know you must include

You ask:

```text
plan a 7 day Paris trip with a clear day-by-day itinerary, likely costs, and anything I still need to decide
```

You get back:

- `7 Day Paris Trip`
- `Likely Costs`
- `What You Still Need To Decide`
- `Booking Checklist`

Then you do:

- open the itinerary first
- review the missing choices before booking
- keep the checklist visible instead of rebuilding it later

## 4. Choose one option and keep the reasoning attached {#vendor-choice}

You paste:

- several options
- your criteria
- the tradeoffs people are debating

You ask:

```text
compare these options and give me a recommendation I can defend
```

You get back:

- `Recommendation Memo`
- `Tradeoff Table`
- `Questions Still Open`

Then you do:

- review the recommendation
- see where the decision is still weak
- keep the reasoning attached for later review

## 5. Close work cleanly, not just quickly {#incident-closure}

You paste:

- the main problem looks fixed
- people want to move on
- important follow-up may still be missing

You ask:

```text
show me what still has to happen before this is really closed
```

You get back:

- `Closure Checklist`
- `Remaining Risks`
- `Follow-up Owners`

Then you do:

- separate “the pain stopped” from “the work is closed”
- keep cleanup visible
- avoid forgetting the aftercare

## What Should Feel Better Than Plain Chat

Across all five cases, Jini should do three things better than a raw chat:

1. give you something usable quickly
2. keep what is missing visible
3. keep the next step obvious

If it only gives you a cleaner explanation of the work, it is not good enough.
